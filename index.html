The monkeys

The Yucatan black howler monkey (Alouatta pigra), known locally as "mono saraguato," is a diurnal primate that lives in cohesive social groups comprising 2 to 12 individuals. These groups typically consist of one or two adult males, two to four adult females, and their young. Feeding primarily on leaves and ripe fruits found in the tropical forests of southeast Mexico, as well as parts of Guatemala and Belize, they are famous for their loud calls, often emitted collectively, making them among the loudest terrestrial mammals. Unfortunately, like most primate species globally, they are endangered due to deforestation and habitat loss resulting from the conversion of tropical forests into cattle pastures and urban centers. Here are some of the individuals I have gotten to know in detail:

Lumi

Lumi is a gorgeous female that I met in 2019 while collecting data for my Master's. She lives in a rubber plantation with very few trees she can actually feed from. She's a resilient and amazing mother of two.

Carmelo

Carmelo is a proud father of (counting...). He lives in a forest fragment that has been isolated due to a huge oil-palm plantation that now has completely surrounded the edge of his tiny forest.

Canela

Canela is a beautiful aunt of two. She lives in a group that resides in the local primary school of Palenque. While some of the kids love to see the monkeys feeding on their trees, some still don't know yet how amazing this monkeys are and throw rocks at them.

Chato

Chato is a grumpy father of (counting...). He loves mangoes (an introduced fruiting tree in Palenque) and shares family with Canela.

Rosa

Rosa and her baby live in a forest fragment located East of Palenque National Park. While these forest fragments might have a composition of the vegetation more imilar to that of Palenque National Park, the connectivity of her habitat is really reduced.

Polo

Polo is the grumpiest of them all. He lives with Lili and Lula in a small forest fragment surrounded by cattle ranching. While they live alone in this forest fragment, they would love it if it could only be connected to bigger forests.
