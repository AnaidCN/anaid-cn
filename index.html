About me

Hi! I’m Anaid, originally from Mexico City and now a PhD candidate at UC Berkeley. I earned my MSc in Biological Sciences from the National Autonomous University of Mexico and have spent the past ten years studying black howler monkeys! My main research focus is on how primates adjust their behavior and ecology in human-modified habitats.

The monkeys

The Yucatan black howler monkey (Alouatta pigra), known locally as "mono saraguato," is a diurnal primate that lives in cohesive social groups comprising 2 to 12 individuals. These groups typically consist of one or two adult males, two to four adult females, and their young. Feeding primarily on leaves and ripe fruits found in the tropical forests of southeast Mexico, as well as parts of Guatemala and Belize, they are famous for their loud calls, often emitted collectively, making them among the loudest terrestrial mammals. Unfortunately, like most primate species globally, they are endangered due to deforestation and habitat loss resulting from the conversion of tropical forests into cattle pastures and urban centers.
